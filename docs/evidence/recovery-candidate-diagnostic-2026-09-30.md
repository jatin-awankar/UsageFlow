Both containers were loopback-only, newly created, and removed by the harness at exit. The saved report and journal are `/tmp/usageflow-recovery-plan-evidence/d7fb4a9d-70a/volume-evidence.json` and `sender-journal.jsonl` in the same directory.

The exact observed Prisma SQL shape (the no-cursor candidate branch) and the `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` output are in [recovery-candidate-plan-2026-09-30.sql](recovery-candidate-plan-2026-09-30.sql) and [recovery-candidate-plan-2026-09-30.txt](recovery-candidate-plan-2026-09-30.txt). The output records every bound value and the snapshot: 2026-09-30 18:00:04.247607 UTC, database size 38,964,371 bytes, 13,212 accepted events, 10,551 rated events, and 13,212 intents. Redis waiting depth increased from 2,426 to 2,675 across the three seconds before the capture. The sender did not finish until 18:00:49.785 UTC. The plan is a separate `PREPARE`/`EXPLAIN EXECUTE` against the same disposable database, so it uses the observed SQL and parameter types, not the worker's own invocation or session cache.

Execution took 15.154 ms, with 0.778 ms of planning and 2,344 shared buffer hits, no reported physical reads. The largest measured section is the join/filter pipeline before the top-N sort: the hash left join reached 11.754 ms and discarded 10,833 rows after combining all 13,213 eligible-lease intents with two scans of `UsageEvent` for processing state. A third `UsageEvent` scan and hash join applied `billingTreatment`; the sort added roughly 0.2 ms. The three event aliases and late OR filter cause broad reads of rows that cannot become candidates. This single warm-cache execution does **not** establish that the scan is the dominant reason for queue growth or explain the much larger cumulative statement times in the earlier diagnosis; concurrent waits, cursor variants, cache state, and host contention remain unpartitioned.

Compare plans and lost-dispatch/unrated recovery tests before considering adoption. No query, index, or recovery behavior was changed here.
