# Public showcase: visual references and direction proposals

Research date: 2026-10-03. Status: research and direction selection only; neither direction is selected. No prototype, UI implementation, fixture, database change, or deployment is included.

## Scope and method

Read alongside the [approved brief](../.scratch/public-showcase/spec.md) and [backend contract verification](public-showcase-contract-verification.md). Fixtures remain undefined until that verification informs their design.

The agreed showcase has three pages: landing, a single guided demo workspace, and implementation/evidence. It uses independent browser-local synthetic sessions. The primary audience is technical SaaS founders and engineering leads. The approved story and domain constraints remain authoritative; visual design must not add administrative workflows or change status meanings.

Four first-party reference sites were read and their landing viewports inspected in the browser. This was a visual/content study, not a full interaction, responsive, performance, or accessibility audit. The observations below describe the viewed pages; the proposed application to UsageFlow is design judgment. No reference assets, brand identities, screenshots, code, or copy are proposed for reuse. A reference link is not a licence grant. Source designs may change.

## Curated references: what to learn

1. **[Resend](https://resend.com/): editorial confidence for a developer product.** Its inspected hero pairs a large serif headline with quieter explanatory sans text and a short primary/secondary action pair. Learn how a technical product can lead with a human-readable promise and typographic personality. Apply the hierarchy and restraint to Direction A; do not reproduce the dark atmospheric artwork, brand typography, or decorative treatment. Resend's actual fonts are not being identified or licensed here.
2. **[MotherDuck](https://motherduck.com/): distinctive technical presentation.** The inspected viewport uses a warm neutral background, outlined controls, pronounced uppercase headings, and a bordered interface example with labeled tabs. Learn how a clear graphic grammar can make technical material recognizable without gradients or a generic card grid. Apply disciplined rules, captions, and an immediately legible product example to Direction A. Do not borrow its duck identity, illustrations, or AI positioning.
3. **[Linear](https://linear.app/): continuity from promise to product detail.** The inspected hero uses a large sans headline above a substantial product interface; the page content groups examples around work stages. Learn how the product itself can carry visual weight and how consistent alignment links marketing to interface detail. Apply that continuity to Direction B. Avoid reproducing its dark SaaS aesthetic, low-contrast incidental text, broad feature catalog, or full administrative navigation.
4. **[Stripe Billing](https://stripe.com/billing): tangible financial explanations.** The page presents concrete quantities, unit prices, totals, and interface examples alongside outcomes. Learn to make billing claims inspectable through worked numbers. Both directions should show the selected event's contribution beside the monthly total. Do not import payment, tax, subscription, or invoice behavior into UsageFlow's BillingRecord journey, and do not borrow the inspected gradient artwork or Stripe's production claims.

These references are sufficient for the first comparison. Adding more galleries or templates before testing these two proposals would expand research without resolving the key design question.

## Direction A: The Annotated Ledger

**Central idea:** UsageFlow reads like a carefully edited financial publication in which every number has an explanation. The interface's main organizing unit is a chapter of one customer's monthly record.

- **Landing composition:** An asymmetric publication layout: concise masthead, expressive headline on the left, a large typeset event-to-monthly-amount worked example on the right. A narrow strip states “Synthetic data · Local simulation · No signup.” Below it, three short editorial sections explain acceptance, price provenance, and accountable delivery. A compact evidence excerpt points to the evidence page. The hero uses actual explanatory relationships rather than a screenshot collage.
- **Demo workspace:** A vertically progressing document with numbered chapters and a slim sticky chapter index. A persistent month/customer header anchors the scenario. Completed chapters remain expandable; the active chapter has one clear action. Event details and calculation notes appear as adjacent annotations on wide screens and immediately below the relevant section on mobile. Monthly review becomes a generous ledger block rather than a separate dashboard. The explicit UTC time transition appears between the usage chapters and finalization, with period and close instants printed together.
- **Typography:** Propose **Fraunces** for large editorial headings and **Inter** for body, controls, and data. Use restrained Fraunces settings, readable body sizes, tabular numerals, and ordinary case for operational labels. Use a system monospace fallback for IDs/code rather than a third downloaded family. This pairing is a proposal, not an assertion about the reference sites' fonts.
- **Data presentation:** A readable equation for quantity × applicable price; ruled rows for contributions; a single highlighted row for the visitor's event; an exact monthly reconciliation. A small proportional contribution graphic is optional only if it adds understanding beyond the numbers. Label the distinction between raw calculation precision and displayed money according to the verified backend contract. No decorative financial charts.
- **Motion:** Short, user-triggered reveals of annotations and a restrained highlight when the accepted event appears in the monthly record. Preserve layout continuity. No scroll hijacking, autoplay story, counting money animation, or simulated latency for atmosphere. Reduced motion shows the same information immediately.
- **Candidate palette territory:** Warm paper, dark ink, and one confident accent such as vermilion or deep green, selected after contrast testing. This is not a final palette.
- **Why it is distinct:** Reading rhythm, whitespace, editorial hierarchy, and an accumulating document provide the identity. A visitor experiences a narrated case study with inspectable evidence.
- **Risk to test:** It could feel like a report rather than an interactive product, or become too tall. Keep the active action visible, instructions short, and past chapters condensed while preserving details.

## Direction B: The Signal Desk

**Central idea:** UsageFlow is an instrument for tracing one event through a precisely defined system. The interface's main organizing unit is a state transition with its input and output evidence.

- **Landing composition:** A bold sans statement above a broad, purposeful flow diagram. The diagram links a prepared event, its price, a monthly BillingRecord, and delivery; a compact caption explains each output. A narrow outcome statement and one dominant “Explore the demo” action lead into the workspace. Subsequent sections alternate wide technical figures with short explanations. Avoid a floating-window hero and ornamental telemetry.
- **Demo workspace:** A fixed conceptual frame: stage rail across the top, current object/action in the main area, and an evidence inspector beside it. Selecting a completed stage changes the inspected object without resetting the run. A compact persistent summary tracks the same event ID, UTC month, and reconciled total. On mobile, the stage rail becomes a wrapping numbered list and the inspector follows the active object in reading order; no miniature desktop panels. The scenario-time control is a clearly labeled transition with before/after timestamps, not a clock that appears to run in real time.
- **Typography:** Propose **IBM Plex Sans** for display, body, and controls plus **IBM Plex Mono** for timestamps, IDs, payloads, and calculation traces. Contrast large confident headings with precise small annotations, while keeping operational text readable. Avoid terminal-style body copy or all-caps everywhere.
- **Data presentation:** A compact provenance chain with stable references and explicit status labels. Each step pairs human explanation with a selectable structured detail view. Quantities and money align in a calculation strip; the monthly contribution remains visible while inspecting the event. The webhook view shows payload-to-record references and delivery outcome. Lines encode relationships, not throughput or unimplemented concurrency.
- **Motion:** A single brief transition along the active connector when an action completes; evidence updates at the destination. An identical retry visibly returns to the same event and leaves totals fixed. No ambient pulses, endless activity logs, 3D camera movements, or network-traffic theatre. Reduced motion uses a static selected connector and textual update.
- **Candidate palette territory:** Light mineral/white workspace with graphite structure and a precise cobalt or amber accent. A contrasting landing field may be explored, but dark mode and a theme switch are not additional deliverables.
- **Why it is distinct:** Spatial continuity, a simultaneous evidence inspector, diagrams, and state-oriented hierarchy provide the identity. A visitor experiences a compact explanatory instrument rather than a document.
- **Risk to test:** It could become too dense or imply an operational control plane. Keep one active object and action, use plain-language stage captions, and retain the simulation label beside the frame.

## Small, coherent resource foundation

The existing [package manifest](../package.json) already declares Tailwind, Radix, Lucide, and Recharts alongside React/Next. The recommendation is to reuse that foundation, with Radix as the sole complex interaction primitive layer for new showcase work. Do not introduce another kit, complete dashboard template, or animation framework for this comparison. Existing unrelated screens need not be migrated.

Licences below were read from official upstream repositories on 2026-10-03. These are upstream source checks, not a complete dependency/transitive-asset audit or verification of every installed package version. Before shipping, pin chosen versions/files, check their distributed licences, and preserve notices in the repository/distribution.

- **Tailwind CSS — MIT.** Reuse styling infrastructure and define a small original token set. The licence permits reuse/modification with its copyright and permission notices retained. It does not license separate commercial Tailwind templates. [Official licence](https://github.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **Radix Primitives — MIT.** Use only needed dialog/disclosure/tab primitives; ordinary buttons, inputs, and tables can remain semantic HTML. Retain copyright/permission notices. Radix documents keyboard and focus behavior, but correct labels, reading order, visual contrast, and application testing remain our responsibility; adopting it is not proof of WCAG conformance. [Official licence](https://github.com/radix-ui/primitives/blob/main/LICENSE), [accessibility guidance](https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/overview/accessibility).
- **Lucide — ISC, with MIT terms for listed Feather-derived icons.** Reuse a small icon subset with textual labels where meaning matters. Preserve the complete relevant licence notices rather than labeling the whole collection simply “MIT.” [Official licence and derived-icon list](https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **Recharts — MIT, optional.** Reuse only if a real quantitative chart survives design review. The event flow itself can be semantic HTML and original SVG; a charting library is not needed to draw a process. Retain notices; supply textual data equivalents and verify keyboard/screen-reader behavior in the chosen implementation. [Official licence](https://github.com/recharts/recharts/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **Fraunces — SIL OFL 1.1; Inter — SIL OFL 1.1.** Direction A candidates. Free embedding and redistribution are permitted subject to the licences; retain each font's copyright and OFL text, do not sell font files alone, and review conditions before modifying/subsetting. Self-host only the selected files/weights. [Fraunces official licence](https://github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces/blob/master/OFL.txt), [Inter official licence](https://github.com/rsms/inter/blob/master/LICENSE.txt).
- **IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono — SIL OFL 1.1.** Direction B candidates within one family project. Preserve copyright and OFL text; “Plex” is a Reserved Font Name, which matters for modified versions. Use official unmodified selected font files initially. [IBM Plex official licence](https://github.com/IBM/plex/blob/master/LICENSE.txt).

**Templates:** No template is proposed for reuse. Both concepts need original composition and only a few shared controls; a full admin template would import irrelevant routes, visual conventions, and dependencies. If a future candidate is proposed, require a direct licence for that exact template and bundled assets, responsive/keyboard inspection, and a clear reduction in work. “Free download” alone is insufficient.

## Evaluation and next decision

Recommend taking **both directions** into the already-requested visual comparison, with Direction A as the initial identity hypothesis and Direction B as a genuine alternative. Neither is approved for implementation now. The next stage should present each direction's landing page and main demo screen using the same domain-verified scenario, content, and information, so visual differences are not confounded with feature differences.

Evaluate each future prototype against five questions:

1. **Comprehension:** Can a first-time viewer explain UsageFlow's purpose and find the next demo action? Can they distinguish acceptance from later processing/rating?
2. **Traceability:** Can they connect the unchanged event ID, applicable price, monthly contribution, BillingRecord, and webhook without assistance?
3. **Distinctiveness:** Does the composition have an identifiable visual grammar beyond color and rounded cards, while remaining believable for a billing product?
4. **Accessibility/responsiveness:** Does the reading order survive mobile, keyboard, zoom, and reduced motion? Document automated and manual findings and limitations against the WCAG 2.2 AA target; do not infer full conformance from a tool score.
5. **Feasibility:** Can it be built within the existing stack and zero-spend constraints without large new dependencies, paid assets, or administrative scope expansion?

For resource selection, additionally inspect exact-version licence/notice obligations, actual package and asset payload, maintenance evidence, existing-stack compatibility, customization cost, keyboard/focus behavior, and absence of paid-service dependencies. No bundle-size or maintenance-score claims have been measured during this research.

The usability target remains **4 of 5 first-time evaluators completing within five minutes**, pending actual participant sessions. Record each participant's completion, time, assistance, and comprehension observations; there are no participant results yet. The contact destination, final font files, final palette, hosting verification, fixtures, and accessibility/usability validation remain pending at their appropriate later stages.
